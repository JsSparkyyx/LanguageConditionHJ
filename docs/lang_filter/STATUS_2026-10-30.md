# Language-conditioned safety filter: where the project stands (2026-10-30)

This page describes the project from scratch: what the method is, how the model is built, how it is trained and
what came out. All the numbers are read from the run directories on 2026-10-30 (`runs/lang/rounds_*`). On
2026-10-30 every round training was stopped because the results are bad; the model is to be analyzed and
changed before anything is retrained. A second, separate line of work (fine-tuning a VLA policy on safe
demonstrations) is still running and is summarized in section 8.

## 0. Summary in a few lines

- We train a **safety filter**: a learned value function Q(s, a, sentence) that predicts, from camera images,
  the robot state and a sentence such as "don't knock over the tall red block", whether the rule in the
  sentence will be broken. When Q of the planner's next action is below 0, a learned safe actor takes over.
- Trained with HJ-reachability SAC (the CrossSafe recipe) plus counterfactual relabeling of the sentence,
  on 5 robot tasks in simulation (RoboTwin / SAPIEN, one dual-arm UR5).
- **Offline numbers look good** (on training batches the filter ranks the harmful rule below the harmless one
  98% of the time), but **in closed loop the filter does not work**:
  - with the filter on, the named rule is still broken in about half of the episodes where the item is in
    the way (47%, vs about 70% for the planner alone);
  - the robot stalls: 77% of those episodes and 50% of the harmless ones run out of time (the planner alone
    never runs out of time);
  - the filter also engages in 54% of the episodes where the named item is never at risk.
- **What the logs point to** (section 6):
  1. the critic ignores the action: two random actions anywhere in the allowed range change Q by about
     0.01% of |Q|;
  2. so the actor gets no learning signal and outputs nearly the same command in every state;
  3. long runs collapse to "everything is unsafe" (Q < 0 everywhere, the filter drives 90-100% of the time);
  4. on scenes it was not trained on, the filter cannot tell which object the sentence names (earlier
     finding: about 0.5-0.6 on object pairs, close to chance).

## 1. The problem

### 1.1 Robot, simulator and the careless planner

- RoboTwin 2.0 tasks in SAPIEN 3, one embodiment: `ur5-wsg`, two UR5 arms (6 joints each) with WSG grippers.
- Physics at 250 Hz, control at 25 Hz: one **tick** = 0.04 s = 10 physics steps. Episodes are capped at 750
  ticks (30 s).
- The **nominal controller** is a cuRobo motion planner made careless on purpose: it ignores the items it must
  protect, so it often breaks a rule. It sends a per-tick joint displacement a_nom.
- **Action** a = δθ: per-joint displacement for one tick, bounded per joint by
  dtheta_max = (joint velocity limit) × 0.04 s. The filter never drives the grippers.

### 1.2 The five tasks used

| task | the robot's job | what can go wrong | rules that can be named |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 `selective_avoid` | pick and place (5 host tasks) with 2-3 extra objects around (wine glass, milk box, apple, bottle, coloured box, coloured cylinder) | the arm or the carried object touches or moves an object | `avoid` X (don't touch), `stay` X (don't move it) |
| T3 `push_zone` | push a box into a frame past coloured mats and standing cylinders | the box crosses a mat; a cylinder is knocked or moved | `zone_exclude` per mat, `upright` and `stay` per cylinder |
| T5 `tray_transport` | slide a tray carrying a tall and a short block | a fast stop tips the tall block | `upright` per block |
| T6 `clean_pour` | pour beads from a mug into one of three bowls (white, blue, green) | beads land in a non-target bowl | `exclude_particles` per non-target bowl |
| T7 `gap_place` | slide a block into a narrow gap between neighbours | a neighbour is scraped, moved or knocked | `upright` and `stay` per neighbour |

- In T1 the item is coupled to the robot (touching is a distance), which a hand-written barrier function could
  also handle; T3, T5, T6 and T7 are the cases the method is for: the item moves on its own after contact
  (sliding, tipping, flowing beads), so the danger cannot be computed from the robot state alone.
- Each scene layout decides which items are **in the way** of the careless motion. A named rule is
  **harm-designated** if its item is in the way, **benign** if not. Example (T6): the careless pour is aimed
  16-22 cm off the target, toward one of the two other bowls; naming that bowl is a harm run, naming the
  other bowl is a benign run.

### 1.3 Failure value h and sentences

- One scale for every rule: **h = clip(margin / unit, −1, 1)**: +1 = comfortably safe, 0 = the rule is
  just broken, −1 = badly broken. Units: 5 cm for distances and zones; 45° for `upright`; 1.5 cm (T3), 2 cm
  (T7) or 3 cm (T1) for `stay`. Bead rules count beads: n beads in the wrong bowl give
  h = −(0.5 + 0.5·(min(n, 20) − 1)/19), so one bead reads −0.5 and 20 or more read −1.
  Example: 3 beads in the blue bowl under "don't spill any beads into the blue bowl" give h = −0.553.
- h is computed from simulator state at every tick (training labels only; the deployed filter sees only
  cameras, joint angles and the sentence).
- Sentences: 5 plain sentences per rule template (4 for training, 1 held out), filled with the item's words
  ("the tall red block", "the glass"), whose referent phrase must pick out one item in the scene. Held-out
  splits: unseen templates, unseen nouns, unseen object models and colours. **No rule type or task type is
  ever given to the model; the sentence is the only rule description.**

### 1.4 The filter at deployment

At each tick: encode the cameras, compute Q(s, a_nom, sentence) with the min of two critic twins. If Q < 0
the safe actor drives for 3 ticks (re-queried every tick); at the end of each 3-tick block the filter
releases if Q(s, a_nom) ≥ 0 again. The actor's command is its deterministic action (the mean, no noise).

## 2. Model architecture (the "vanilla" model, run `rounds_ur5_g095_1023`)

Dimensions: every token is 256 numbers.

**Frozen front end (HoloBrain-0 GD, a pretrained VLA's encoder; nothing here is trained):**
- **Scene**: 3 camera images (left wrist, right wrist, head) with depth → 1200 tokens per frame, each with
  its own 3D world position from the depth map. Three frames are used: now (t), 2 ticks ago (t−2) and the
  episode start (0) → 3600 scene tokens. A shared trainable adapter plus a learned per-frame time embedding
  maps them into the model.
- **Robot**: CrossSafe's `RobotInjection`: 22 body tokens (20 robot links + 2 held-object slots), each with
  the link's position and its Jacobians (how the link moves when each joint moves).
- **Text**: HoloBrain's frozen BERT + its `text_feat_map` → up to 24 word tokens. A trainable 2-layer
  self-attention adapter runs over [r, q₁..q₄, words + position embedding] and gives: r₀ (a sentence
  summary), 4 readout queries q₁..q₄, and adapted words w′.

**Trainable trunk:**
- The scene tokens read the words once (cross-attention, residual), then stay fixed.
- 3 blocks, each with 5 pre-norm residual sub-layers:
  - (a) body tokens read the scene, with CrossSafe's geometric attention bias (log distance and direction
    between each link and each scene token);
  - (b) body tokens read the words;
  - (c) the 4 queries read the scene (no geometric bias);
  - (d) self-attention over [22 body tokens, r, 4 queries];
  - (e) FiLM from r₀ on body and queries (zero-initialized), then a feed-forward layer.
- Language reaches Q through five paths (scene-reads-words, body-reads-words, FiLM, queries, the r token),
  each with an on/off switch for ablations. All five were on.

**Actor** (`LangTokenActor`): for each joint, the joint's body token → RMSNorm → Linear(256, 256) → SiLU →
Linear(256, 2) = (μ, log σ), one head shared by all joints (66,562 weights). Command
δθ = tanh(μ + σ·ε) × dtheta_max, with log σ clamped to [−5, 0]. The actor reads the trunk's output with
gradients cut off, so the actor loss trains only this head.

**Twin critics** (two copies, each):
1. **Action injection**: the joint command δθ is turned into per-link motion: dp_i = J_lin,i·δθ and
   dω_i = J_ang,i·δθ. Per link, the 8 numbers [δθ of its own joint, dp (3), dω (3), valid flag] go through
   Linear(8, 256) and are added to that link's body token.
2. **Post-action geometry**: the geometric bias uses the link positions after the move (p + dp).
3. 2 more blocks with the same 5 sub-layers.
4. **Readout** (`softmin`, used by every round run): a value head on each of the 22 body tokens and 4
   queries, Q = softmin over these 26 values with temperature 0.05 (in h units). Contact rules are expected
   to be carried by a link token, spill / tilt / movement / zone rules by a query. (An MLP readout exists in
   the code but was not used by these runs.)

Q is in h units, so Q ≈ +1 means "safe for the foreseeable future" and Q < 0 means "the rule will be broken".

**Variant, HoloBrain-Qwen front end** (run `rounds_ur5_qwen_g095_1023`): the same trainable model on
HoloBrain's Qwen2.5-VL variant (vision tower of Qwen2.5-VL-3B plus its first language layer, frozen). It gives
297 scene tokens per frame (3 cameras × 9 × 11 grid) and its word tokens have already attended over the image
tokens. It was added to test whether a front end pretrained on image-text pairs helps the model find the
named object (see 5.5).

## 3. Training recipe

### 3.1 Losses (HJ-SAC, as in CrossSafe)

- **Critic target**: y = (1 − γ)·h(s) + γ·min(h(s), ½(Q̄₁ + Q̄₂)(s′, π(s′))), with γ = 0.95. Q̄ are the
  slowly updated target copies (Polyak τ = 0.005); π(s′) is a sample of the current actor at the next
  state. This makes Q estimate a discounted "worst h from now on".
- **Critic loss**: MSE(Q₁, y) + MSE(Q₂, y), evaluated at the action actually taken in the data. It trains
  the critics, the trunk, the text adapter and the scene adapter.
- **Actor loss**: mean of −min(Q₁, Q₂)(s, a_π) + α·log π(a_π | s) + 0.001·(pre-tanh value)², on detached
  trunk features (trains the actor head only). α anneals from 0.2 to 0.02 over the first 20k steps.
- AdamW, lr 3e-4, batch 64, gradient clipping at norm 10, TF32 matmuls in the training step.

### 3.2 Relabeling (the language training signal)

Every stored tick carries the h of every rule of its episode, plus a `fails_soon` bit per rule (h < 0
within the next 25 ticks). Each training sample draws one rule of its episode and one training sentence for
it, and the target uses that rule's h.
- **harmful** at a tick = that rule's `fails_soon` bit is set; **benign** otherwise.
- Batches hold 50% harmful and 50% benign rules, balanced across tasks.
- 50% of each batch is **paired**: the same transition twice, once with a harmful rule and once with a
  benign one. Example (T7): the same frame with "keep the tall blue block upright" (its h goes to −1 soon)
  and "don't knock over the green brick" (stays +1).

### 3.3 Data and rounds

- **Warmup**: 150 episodes of the careless planner alone (30 per task), all rules recorded
  (`data/lang_1023/warmup`, collected 10-25 / 10-26).
- **Rounds**: round 0 trains on the warmup. Each later round:
  1. collects 4 new episodes with the current weights: 80% with the filter on (one named rule per episode,
     harm-designated with probability 0.5), 20% planner only;
  2. trains 2048 gradient steps on warmup + all collected episodes.
- Planned: 100 rounds (204,800 steps).
- Action coverage: in planner-only episodes, a tick is replaced by a uniform random command in the allowed
  box with probability 0.05 × 10 / 60 ≈ 0.8% (`perturb_prob` 0.05, the rate of an older per-window
  setting). The warmup has no random commands beyond these. Filter episodes add the actor's commands.
- The stored action of each tick is the command actually sent (planner, actor or perturbation).

### 3.4 Runs of the current generation (started 10-27, stopped 10-30)

All γ = 0.95, softmin readout, MLP actor head, ur5-wsg, no scene fusion:

## 4. How the results are measured

From the filter episodes the training collects every round (`eval/rounds.jsonl` in each run):
- **harm**: the named rule was broken in the episode (h < 0 at some tick);
- **success**: the task finished within 30 s;
- **timeout**: the episode reached the 30 s cap;
- **driven ticks**: share of ticks in which the actor, not the planner, drove;
- **engaged**: the filter took over at least once.

Reference: the **planner alone** (careless nominal, warmup episodes), whose harm rate on harm-designated rules
is what the filter should push down, while keeping the planner's success.

From the training batches (`train_log.jsonl`, logged every 20 steps):
- **pair rank accuracy**: share of paired samples where Q is lower for the harmful sentence than for the
  benign one;
- **precision / recall**: predicted unsafe (Q < 0) vs h < 0 at the next tick.

These batch numbers come from training scenes, not held-out ones.

### 5.5 Diagnostics of the critic and the actor (last ~10 rounds)

| run | Q change between two random actions, relative to \|Q\| | spread of the actor's action across states (rad / tick) | spread of the data's actions (rad / tick) |
|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla | 0.00009 | 0.00035 | 0.0094 |
| Qwen | 0.00000 (0.0011 early) | 0.00002 | 0.0078 |
| T1 only | 0.00009 | 0.00023 | 0.0081 |

- "Q change": for a batch of states, draw two random actions anywhere in the allowed box and compare Q; the
  mean difference is 0.01% of |Q| or less. **Q does not depend on the action.**
- The actor's mean action barely varies from state to state, 27 to 450 times less than the commands in the
  data; its gradient norm fell from 0.31 to 0.03 (vanilla).
- The policy's entropy stays flat at 8.2 from the first round to the last, so its noise never narrows. The
  stochastic actor is used only inside the critic target; the filter uses the mean, which carries no
  information.
- Achieved motion / commanded motion per tick is about 0.45 in the GD runs.

### 5.7 Earlier offline studies of grounding (10-08 to 10-14, 7-task scenes)

Question: does the filter find the object the sentence names? Metric: rank accuracy on object pairs, i.e.
the same frame, two sentences of the same kind naming two different objects, one of which is already being
harmed.
- Training scenes: 0.92-1.00. New scenes: 0.49-0.57 (chance is 0.5). The name → object link is
  **memorized per training scene**.
- None of these fixes helped on new scenes:
  - text fixes (normalizing the text outputs, hiding [CLS] / [SEP]): 0.59;
  - an extra loss predicting the named object's 3D centre: 0.60, also memorized;
  - an oracle tag marking the true object: 0.58, the model ignores it;
  - HoloBrain's frozen text-image fusion stage: 0.56 vs 0.57 without it, and its tokens do not single out the
    named object (enrichment 1.32, gate ≥ 2; contrast 0.49, gate ≥ 0.7);
  - more training scenes (16 → 119): 0.49 → 0.57, flat.
- Linear probe (10-14, `probe_report_2026-10-14.md`):
  - the named object's own scene tokens contain its distance to the arm: picked with the true object mask,
    a linear read-out ranks 99-100% of near / far pairs right, also for unseen objects;
  - the model's own summaries rank only 60-65%, about as well as the words alone (64%).
  - So the information is in the frozen tokens; the step from the sentence to the right tokens is what fails.
- The Qwen front end (5.2) was meant to test whether a front end pretrained on image-text pairs fixes this.
  Its grounding on new scenes was never measured: the closed-loop evaluator does not support Qwen yet, and its
  round run collapsed.

## 6. What the numbers point to

These are readings of the logs, not verified causes.

1. **The critic ignores the action, so the filter has nothing useful to do when it engages.**
   - With Q independent of a, the actor's −Q term gives no gradient. Only the entropy term and the small
     penalty on the pre-tanh value act, and that penalty pulls the mean toward 0.
   - So the deterministic safe action is probably close to "don't move". Its size was not logged: load a
     checkpoint to confirm.
   - A filter that can only stop explains the timeouts. While stopped, the state barely changes, so
     Q(s, a_nom) stays < 0 and the filter never releases.
   - It also explains T5, where stopping is the harm.
   - Possible reasons the action is ignored:
     - one tick's effect is tiny (δθ ≈ 0.01 rad, dp of a few mm) and enters raw through Linear(8, 256),
       next to body tokens of size about 1;
     - most stored actions come from the planner and are a function of the state, so the state alone
       already predicts y; random commands are only about 0.8% of planner-only ticks;
     - at 25 Hz with γ = 0.95, one action barely changes the discounted worst h.
2. **Long runs drift to "everything unsafe".** Mean Q falls below h and keeps falling: Qwen −0.64, γ 0.98
   −0.83, the 100-round run −0.23. Once Q < 0 everywhere the filter drives every tick, and the new data are
   ticks of a stalled robot. Candidates:
   - the pessimistic target (min with h, min over twins, the softmin readout is ≤ the minimum) bootstrapped
     through an actor that does not move;
   - the shrinking share of planner data.
3. **Grounding does not generalize** to new scenes (5.7). Even with a working actor, the filter would not
   know which object a sentence names in a scene it has not seen.
4. **The training-batch numbers do not predict the closed loop.** 0.98 rank accuracy and 0.99 recall in
   training sit next to a filter that stalls in closed loop. The batch metrics come from training scenes and
   do not test the action.
5. The closed-loop samples are small: 8-18 episodes per task and designation in a run. Differences below
   about 0.2 within one task are noise.

## 7. VLM as the failure function (prepared, not run)

Idea: replace the simulator's h (not available on a real robot) by a VLM that looks at an agent-view frame
and the sentence and answers whether the rule is broken.
- Selection (10-27 to 10-29, `VLM_FAILURE_PLAN.md`):
  - score per task = (share of broken ticks caught + 1 − false-alarm share) / 2, averaged over T1, T5, T6;
  - Qwen2.5-VL-32B in 8 bits: 0.851 (T1 0.777, T5 0.921, T6 0.854);
  - RoboBrain2.0-32B in 8 bits: 0.764;
  - Qwen in 16 bits: 0.833.
- Weak spot: T1 touches. Per tick, Qwen catches 33% of the touches and only 12% of its "touched" answers are
  right.
- The training run with Qwen labels (25 rounds × 16 episodes × 8192 steps, one job with one GPU training and
  one labeling) was cancelled on 10-30 before it started, with every other round training.

## 8. Separate line: VLA fine-tuned on safe demonstrations (still running)

Goal: a policy that does the five tasks safely by itself, without a filter.
- **Model**: HoloBrain-0 GD, starting from its RoboTwin post-trained checkpoint
  (`post_training_robotwin`), predicting 64 future steps per call.
- **Data**: 500 safe demonstrations, 100 per task (T1: 20 per host task). They were collected with new safe
  modes of the planner that protect every item:
  - T1: cuRobo with every object in its collision world;
  - T3: a route around the mats and cylinders;
  - T5: a slower drag;
  - T6: an aimed, low, slow pour;
  - T7: a centred slide.
  A demo is kept only if the task succeeded and no rule was broken (T1: no contact at all).
- **One model for all five tasks**, 1 A40 GPU, batch 8 × 4 gradient accumulation = 32, 15,000 steps.
  - AdamW, lr 1e-4 (backbone 1e-5), weight decay 5e-4;
  - linear warm-up over 500 steps, lr × 0.1 at step 13,500.
  - Trained in 1-hour jobs that resume from the newest checkpoint.
- **Status on 10-30 12:15**: step 6,000 of 15,000; the training loss fell from 1.2 to about 0.17. A disk
  outage cost about 2.5 hours that morning. Expected end: early 10-31.
- **Test plan** (automatic):
  - the base and the fine-tuned model, 20 unseen scenes per task: success, rule broken, T1 contact, item
    thrown, and safe success = success with nothing harmed;
  - if safe success is below 0.80, the step-7,500 checkpoint is tested too;
  - if the 15k model beats it by ≥ 0.10, training continues 15k more steps from the 15k weights, and again
    while each extension gains ≥ 0.05, up to 45k steps.
